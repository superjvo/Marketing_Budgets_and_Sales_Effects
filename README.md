# Marketing Budgets and Sales Effects
Using data from numerous marketing promotions to predict sales impact based on advertising investment
# Overview
In this project the goal was to create an accurate sales prediction model in relation to marketing budgets with multiple linear regression. The data used in this project was the historical marketing promotional data for a fictional small business. The resulting model was able to attribute 90.3% of sales variation to the various advertisement strategies and provided a solid baseline to forecast future performance. The coefficients of the model pointed towards TV promotion as the primary factor that drove sales followed by radio. Social media surprisingly showed a negligible effect on the business's financial outcome.    
# Business Understanding
Digital and online advertising have become an integral part of marketing promotional strategies often comprising of over 68% of global advertising spend. While the size of the business does play a role in the utilization of this medium, most companies in the modern era benefit from the wider scope and ease of accessibility provided through these methods. The digitization of marketing has been trending upward since the early 2000s coinciding with the advent of google ads and has expanded rapidly in the mobile marketing era that can be attributed to smartphone applications and social media.
# Data Understanding
The historical marketing promotion data for this project consisted of 572 observations with 5 distinct variables. The Sales variable was continuous as was radio. Categorical features were used to describe TV budget as low, medium, and high. The social media was split into two variables, one listing the promotional investment as continuous with the other categorizing influencers into nano, micro, macro, and mega based on follower count. # Modeling and Evaluation
A regression model was fit utilizing all continuous variables and a single categorical variable which was TV. The influencer variable was dropped from the model because the average sales for each category were all in a relatively short range of values indicating little to no contribution to sales. The model was fit with an ordinary least squares regressor which returned an adjusted R-Squared of 0.903 indicating 90.3% of the variation in sales can be attributed to the variables used in this model. With a high TV budget being the baseline for that category, the intercept coefficient of 218.65 indicates with all other variables removed the sales for a high tv budget can be expected to be 218.65 million dollars and a 95% confidence interval ranging from 206.29-231.00. Additional TV category coefficients were -75.33 (confidence: -82.45, -68.20) for the medium budget and -154.31 (confidence: -164.00, -144.62. These statistics point towards a substantial decrease in sales when lowering the TV investment as dropping to a medium budget resulted in 75.33 million dollars and the lowest budget being 154.31 million dollars less in sales when compared to high budgets. The radio coefficient of 2.99 with a confidence interval between 2.53-3.45 shows for every million dollars increase to the radio budget sales can be expected to rise by around 3 million. Social medias coefficient was much lower compared to the other variables at -0.15 with a confidence range between -1.47-1.17. While this does suggest a negative correlation to sales, the confidence interval is inconclusive, and social media was the only variable that did not return a statistically significant P-value. # Conclusion
The linear regression model incorporated in this project can effectively forecast sales projections based on marketing promotion investment as currently constructed. The deeper understanding how these factors interact provided insights that may be even more important than the current models prediction capability. Even though social media had little to no impact on sales this finding is also worth noting and can be used to improve profitability. The additional marketing budget of social media campaigns could be allocated to one of the other mediums that have shown a more direct correlation to sales to boost revenue. Further steps can be taken to facilitate greater model performance with the most immediate revision being fit on the variables excluding social media. Long term improvements to optimize the model can also be achieved through the incorporation of more granular TV budget data. Gaining access to specific TV promotion amounts would vastly increase the model's predictive power. It would provide a more precise measurement of the TV coefficient, allowing the business to accurately forecast how changes in TV investment impact sales.
